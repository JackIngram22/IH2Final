Get Involved

Support the movements working to end mass incarceration

The criminal justice system in the U.S. is deeply rooted in policies that perpetuate racial and economic inequality. But these systems are not immovable. They are shaped by laws, funding, and public pressure and they can be reshaped by people demanding better.

You don’t need to be a politician or a lawyer to make a difference. Educators, students, organizers, artists, and everyday citizens have always played a role in criminal justice reform. Here are some of the leading organizations doing the work, and how you can support them:

  • The Marshall Project — A nonprofit newsroom covering the U.S. criminal justice system through investigative journalism. Their reporting brings national attention to systemic abuse, wrongful convictions, and policy failures.
  • Vera Institute of Justice — Works with governments and communities to end mass incarceration, with a focus on racial equity, public safety alternatives, and reentry support.
  • Equal Justice Initiative (EJI) — Provides legal representation to people who have been wrongly convicted, unfairly sentenced, or abused in jails and prisons. EJI also promotes historical education through the Legacy Museum and National Memorial for Peace and Justice.
  • Critical Resistance — A grassroots abolitionist organization committed to dismantling the prison-industrial complex. They focus on community organizing, anti-policing efforts, and transformative justice models.

Want to go further? Here are a few ways to get involved personally:

  • Support bail funds or mutual aid groups in your city
  • Volunteer to help returning citizens with housing, jobs, or reentry services
  • Educate others — host events, create art, or share content about restorative justice and abolition
  • Push your school, company, or local government to divest from policing and invest in care

The system won’t change on its own. But when people organize, speak up, and build alternatives, change becomes possible. Start where you are. Use what you have. Do what you can.
